The periodic table

All 118 elements. Colour the table by any property, or set a temperature to see what melts and boils. Select an element for its full profile: electron configuration, isotopes, spectrum, and a live description from Wikipedia and PubChem.

Reading the table

Each row, or period, begins when electrons start filling a new shell, and each column, or group, collects elements whose outermost electrons sit in the same arrangement. That is why elements in a column behave alike: lithium, sodium and potassium each have a single loosely held outer electron, and all three react violently with water. The table’s odd shape, two columns on the left, ten in the middle, six on the right and fourteen in the separated rows, is the shape of quantum mechanics itself. Chapter 5 builds it up electron by electron.

Try Property → Ionisation energy and watch the trend chart: the energy needed to remove an electron peaks at every noble gas and collapses at every alkali metal. Or set State to 5,800 K, the temperature of the Sun’s surface, and see which elements could still exist as solids. None can.
